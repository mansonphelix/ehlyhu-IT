Backup & Disaster Recovery Architecture Brief

Data integrity is the bedrock of business continuity. This technical brief explains enterprise backup automation, secondary cloud offsite vaulting, and simulated bare-metal recovery testing procedures.

Immutable Backup Strategy01

EHLYHU constructs backup systems that cannot be altered or deleted by ransomware or insider threat. Immutable snapshots are written to isolated storage with time-locked retention policies that prevent premature deletion. Every backup job is cryptographically verified upon creation and re-validated on a scheduled basis to detect silent data corruption before it matters.

Automated Protection Coverage02

Our backup platform provides continuous protection for workstations, physical servers, virtual machines, and cloud workloads. Incremental backups run on a defined schedule with automated deduplication and compression to minimize storage consumption and network impact. Recovery point objectives are tuned to your data volatility profile, with critical systems protected to near-zero data loss.

Secondary Cloud Vaulting03

Primary backups are replicated to a geographically separate secondary cloud vault that is inaccessible from your production network. This air-gapped replication ensures that even a catastrophic compromise of your primary site leaves recoverable data intact. The secondary vault is monitored for integrity and replication lag, with automated alerting on any deviation from the defined recovery posture.

Documented Recovery Runbooks04

Every protected environment is accompanied by a documented disaster recovery runbook that specifies step-by-step restoration procedures, responsible roles, and target timelines. Runbooks are reviewed quarterly and validated through simulated recovery drills so that your team can execute recovery confidently under pressure without depending on external assistance.

RTO & RPO Targets05

Recovery time and recovery point objectives are defined contractually and measured continuously. Our architecture supports recovery time objectives ranging from fifteen minutes for critical systems to four hours for secondary workloads. Bare-metal recovery orchestration enables rapid failover to replacement hardware without requiring a reimage from scratch.
